\begin{abstract}
We consider ten pairwise non-overlapping circles with radii
$r_i=\sqrt{i}$, $1\le i\le 10$, inside a circular container.  We give a
computer-assisted proof that the minimum container radius is an exact value
$\Rcrit$, defined as the unique solution of two explicit angular equations in
a rational rectangle.  A matching ten-circle realization exists at this
value, and a finite global certificate excludes every smaller radius.  The
lower-bound proof combines a complete radial and angular case tree, a
two-inequality angle barrier for the main seven-circle order, and
negative-cycle certificates for the four exceptional cases.  The complete
composition closes all $14{,}757{,}888$
sector assignments.  The proof decisions in the replay use integer and
rational arithmetic; the result is a reproducible computer-assisted proof,
not an end-to-end theorem-prover formalization.  We do not claim that
$\Rcrit$ is identical to a separately computed algebraic root $R_0$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $r_i=\sqrt{i}$ for $1\le i\le10$.  The problem is to find the smallest
radius $R$ for which there are centers $p_1,\ldots,p_{10}\in\mathbb R^2$
satisfying
\begin{align}
  \norm{p_i}+r_i&\le R
       &&(1\le i\le10), \label{eq:containment}\\
  \norm{p_i-p_j}&\ge r_i+r_j
       &&(1\le i<j\le10). \label{eq:separation}
\end{align}
Write $\Ropt$ for this minimum.  The minimum is attained: after fixing a
bounded interval of candidate radii, all center coordinates lie in a compact
set, and the constraints are closed.

This paper proves exact global optimality, rather than only a lower bound for
a selected subfamily or a local statement near a proposed configuration.  The
proof is a composition of geometric inequalities with finite certificates.
Its central idea is to turn each possible placement into one of finitely many
radial boxes and cyclic-sector assignments.  Most assignments close directly
by rational radial or angular-cycle bounds.  The remaining cases split into
two geometric families:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a seven-circle core in one fixed cyclic order, ruled out by two
        angular sums whose monotonicities point in opposite directions as
        the radius of circle $10$ varies;
  \item four exceptional assignments, projected to a nine-circle order and
        ruled out by a finite negative-cycle certificate.
\end{enumerate}
The angle barrier applies to every residual main-order case by a local
radius estimate from the non-overlap and containment constraints.  Finally,
the same two angular equations that force the lower bound also define a
configuration at $\Rcrit$.

The finite checks are substantial, but the logical roles are simple:
the tree proves that every possible configuration reaches a checked case;
the analytic lemmas prove that each case rule is valid; and the exact witness
proves attainability.  Section~\ref{sec:composition} gives the complete case
accounting, while Section~\ref{sec:verification} describes the replay and
its trust boundary.

\section{Problem and exact statement}

\subsection{Feasibility and the candidate value}

For $\rho>0$, call $\rho$ feasible if there are centers satisfying
\eqref{eq:containment}--\eqref{eq:separation} with $R=\rho$.  Feasibility is
monotone in $\rho$: a configuration feasible at $\rho$ is feasible at every
larger container radius.  Thus a certified infeasibility result at one radius
is a lower bound for $\Ropt$.

We define the exact candidate through the angular quantities introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:barrier}.  Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
  J_t&=[1.0037160860750841,\;1.0037160860750846],\\
  J_R&=[8.3034681221114890,\;8.3034681221114900].
\end{aligned}
\]
The number $\Rcrit$ is the $R$-coordinate of the unique pair
$(t_{\mathrm{crit}},R_{\mathrm{crit}})\in J_t\times J_R$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
  A(t_{\mathrm{crit}},R_{\mathrm{crit}})=2\pi,
  \qquad
  B(t_{\mathrm{crit}},R_{\mathrm{crit}})=2\pi.
  \label{eq:root-definition}
\end{equation}
The existence and uniqueness of this pair, as well as the strict upper
endpoint inequality, are proved by exact rational interval checks in
Section~\ref{sec:barrier}.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
  8.303468122111489\le \Rcrit
  < 8.303468122111490 = \Rcap.
  \label{eq:root-bracket}
\end{equation}
The equations, rather than a rounded decimal or a polynomial root
identification, are the definition of the exact value in this paper.

\begin{theorem}[Global optimality]
\label{thm:global}
For ten circles of radii $1,\sqrt2,\ldots,\sqrt{10}$ in a circular
container,
\[
  \boxed{\Ropt=\Rcrit.}
\]
More precisely, no packing exists for any $R<\Rcrit$, and a packing exists
at $R=\Rcrit$.
\end{theorem}

The exact v27 radial tree covers every configuration with $R\le\Rcap$.
Its residual cases are closed by the angle barrier and the nine-circle
negative-cycle certificate.  Since $\Rcrit<\Rcap$, these facts cover every
putative packing below $\Rcrit$.  The matching construction is given in
Section~\ref{sec:witness}.

\subsection{Exactness and scope}

The value in Theorem~\ref{thm:global} is an exact root-defined real number,
not a claim that the finite decimal in \eqref{eq:root-bracket} is itself the
optimum.  Nor do we identify it with the distinct algebraic number $R_0$
appearing in earlier computations.  The certified interval is narrow enough
to distinguish the exact target used here, and the geometric definition
already suffices to state and prove the optimality result.

\section{How the global proof is organized}
\label{sec:overview}

A packing has ten radial coordinates $s_i=\norm{p_i}$ and ten center
directions.  The proof infrastructure first subdivides the allowed radial
domain by rational midpoint splits.  On each radial box, exact necessary
conditions determine which cyclic orders and angular-sector assignments
remain possible.  These assignments form the finite ledger called the
global radial tree.

At a terminal case, one of the following happens:
\begin{itemize}
  \item interval-safe radial inequalities contradict containment or
        non-overlap;
  \item lower bounds on pair angles contradict the $2\pi$ angular budget,
        often through a Farkas multiplier certificate;
  \item a difference-constraint graph contains a strictly negative directed
        cycle;
  \item the assignment has the main seven-circle order and radial range, so
        the two-sum angle barrier applies;
  \item the assignment is one of the four alternative masks, so its
        nine-circle projection is covered by the negative-cycle tree.
\end{itemize}
Every split is rational and its children cover the parent box exactly.  Thus
the finite tree is a cover argument, not a sampling of candidate
configurations.

The global cap is
\[
  \Rcap=8.303468122111490.
\]
Because the exact root lies strictly below this cap, it is enough for the
tree to cover all configurations up to $\Rcap$; no comparison of floating
approximations is used to bridge a gap.

\section{The main seven-circle angle barrier}
\label{sec:barrier}

\subsection{Radial and angular necessary conditions}

Consider the seven-circle core
\[
  K=\{2,5,6,7,8,9,10\},\qquad
  W=K\setminus\{10\}=\{2,5,6,7,8,9\}.
\]
For a center $p_i$, put $s_i=\norm{p_i}$.  For any two circles with
$r_i+r_j\le s_i+s_j$ and $|s_i-s_j|<r_i+r_j$, define their necessary
smaller central angle by
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{ij}(s_i,s_j)=
  \arccos\!\left(
    \frac{s_i^2+s_j^2-(r_i+r_j)^2}{2s_is_j}
  \right).
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
The law of cosines and non-overlap imply that the directed angular gap from
one center direction to another is at least this smaller angle.  In a
cyclic order, all directed gaps sum to $2\pi$.

The residual main order is
\[
  (10,5,7,9,2,8,6).
\]
For $i\in W$ put
\[
 a_i(R)=R-r_i,\qquad D_i=r_{10}+r_i.
\]
When the six circles in $W$ are at the container wall, the angle between
circle $10$ at radial coordinate $t=s_{10}$ and circle $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i(t,R)=
  \arccos\!\left(
    \frac{t^2+a_i(R)^2-D_i^2}{2t\,a_i(R)}
  \right).
  \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
For two wall circles $i,j\in W$, their necessary angle is
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{ij}(R)=
  2\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{r_ir_j}{a_i(R)a_j(R)}}.
  \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{align}
 C(R)&=\beta_{79}(R)+\beta_{92}(R)+\beta_{28}(R)+\beta_{86}(R),\\
 A(t,R)&=\alpha_5(t,R)+\beta_{57}(R)+C(R)+\alpha_6(t,R),\\
 B(t,R)&=\alpha_7(t,R)+C(R)+\alpha_6(t,R).
 \label{eq:AB}
\end{align}

For the stated order, the directed loop
$10\to5\to7\to9\to2\to8\to6\to10$ has total angle $2\pi$ and therefore
requires $A_{\mathrm{radial}}\le2\pi$.  A second loop
$10\to7\to9\to2\to8\to6\to10$ skips circle $5$ in the angle count, but
circle $5$ remains present in the packing.  It gives the independent
necessary inequality $B_{\mathrm{radial}}\le2\pi$.  These two inequalities
are the key: their dependence on $t$ has opposite signs near the critical
pair.

\subsection{The exact critical pair}

The following derivative formulas explain how the candidate is isolated.
For $i\in\{5,6,7\}$ let
\[
 b_i=a_i(R)^2-D_i^2,\qquad
 H_i=4a_i(R)^2t^2-(t^2+b_i)^2.
\]
Where the angles are in $(0,\pi)$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\alpha_i}{\partial t}
   =\frac{b_i-t^2}{t\sqrt{H_i}},\qquad
 \frac{\partial\alpha_i}{\partial R}
   =-\frac{a_i(R)^2+D_i^2-t^2}{a_i(R)\sqrt{H_i}}.
 \label{eq:alpha-derivatives}
\end{equation}
For a wall-wall angle,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\beta_{ij}}{\partial R}
  =-\left(\frac1{a_i(R)}+\frac1{a_j(R)}\right)
    \sqrt{\frac{r_ir_j}{a_i(R)a_j(R)-r_ir_j}}.
 \label{eq:beta-derivative}
\end{equation}
Exact sign checks establish $A_t>0$ and $B_t<0$ throughout $t\in I$ when
$R\in J_R$.  Separate interval checks establish $A_R<0$ and $B_R<0$ for
$t\in I$ and $\frac{15947}{2000}\le R\le8.30346812212$:
\begin{equation}
  A_t>0,\qquad B_t<0,\qquad A_R<0,\qquad B_R<0.
  \label{eq:root-signs}
\end{equation}
For example, $A_t>0$ follows from $(\alpha_5)_t>0$ and
$(\alpha_6)_t>0$.  The sign of $B_t=(\alpha_7)_t+(\alpha_6)_t$ requires a
comparison of two positive terms; the verifier reduces it to a quadratic
polynomial inequality on the squared interval for $t$.  Its leading
coefficient is negative and its endpoint lower bounds are positive, which
certifies the sign on the full interval.

To prove existence, write
\[
 F(t,R)=A(t,R)-B(t,R)
       =\alpha_5(t,R)+\beta_{57}(R)-\alpha_7(t,R),
 \qquad
 G(t,R)=B(t,R)-2\pi.
\]
The exact checker proves opposite signs for $F$ on the lower and upper
$t$-edges of the rational rectangle, and opposite signs for $G$ on the lower
and upper $R$-edges.  These are whole-edge sign checks: interval bounds on the
relevant derivatives account for variation along each edge.  Since $F_t>0$,
for every $R$ in the rectangle there is exactly one $t(R)$ with $F=0$.
Continuity and the edge signs for $G$ then give an $R$ with $G=0$ by the
intermediate value theorem.  This is precisely a solution of
$A=B=2\pi$.

The solution is unique.  At fixed $R$, $A_t>0$ gives at most one solution
to $A=2\pi$.  If two simultaneous solutions had $R_1<R_2$, then
$A(t_1,R_2)<2\pi$ and $B(t_1,R_2)<2\pi$, because both decrease with $R$.
Restoring $A=2\pi$ at $R_2$ would require increasing $t$, while restoring
$B=2\pi$ would require decreasing $t$, since $A_t>0$ and $B_t<0$.  Both
cannot hold at the same $t$.  Thus the pair is unique.  The exact endpoint
checks further show
\[
  t_{\mathrm{crit}}\in J_t,\qquad
  8.303468122111489\le \Rcrit<8.303468122111490.
\]

All angle and derivative signs just used are checked without floating-point
decisions.  The verifier evaluates rational enclosures for square roots,
uses Machin's identity with alternating arctangent series to enclose $\pi$,
and uses half-angle arctangent expansions with rational remainder bounds for
the inverse trigonometric terms.

\subsection{Why the radial coordinates can be replaced by wall values}

The main order cases do not assume that the circles $i\in W$ are at the wall.
This is important: the theorem does not move disks, and it does not assume
the older sufficient conditions for physically pushing disks outward.

For $d_{ij}=r_i+r_j$, differentiation of \eqref{eq:phi} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\phi_{ij}}{\partial s_i}
  =-\frac{s_i^2-s_j^2+d_{ij}^2}
          {2s_i^2s_j\sin\phi_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:phi-derivative}
\end{equation}
The case boxes imply, for $t=s_{10}\in I=[0.8588,1.1265]$ and
$\frac{15947}{2000}\le R\le\Rcap$,
\[
  r_{10}+r_i-t\le s_i\le \Rcap-r_i
       \qquad(i\in W).
 \label{eq:main-radial-box}
\]
The left inequality follows from
$r_{10}+r_i\le\norm{p_{10}-p_i}\le s_{10}+s_i$; the right one is
containment.  The actual verifier rounds the square-root endpoints outward
to rational bounds before doing its checks.

For most coordinates, \eqref{eq:phi-derivative} has a directly certified
negative sign in each angular sum.  Three shared coordinates require a
combined derivative check because their two adjacent pair contributions
can have opposite signs.  For $(i,g,b)=(9,7,2),(8,6,2),(2,9,8)$, exact
rational bounds give:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
\toprule
coordinate $s_i$ & lower bound for the combined sign expression\\
\midrule
$s_9$ & $>3.193809$\\
$s_8$ & $>0.143756$\\
$s_2$ & $>0.589640$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The smaller angular sines in the two potentially adverse pairs are bounded
below by $3/5$; the remaining terms have positive rational lower bounds.
Consequently both full radial sums are non-increasing in every $s_i$.
The domains of all angles are also checked throughout the substitutions.

Starting from any feasible packing in the main order, replace its six radial
coordinates $s_i$ one at a time by $R-r_i$ only inside the angle formulas.
This is an analytic comparison, not a physical movement.  Since each angular
sum is non-increasing as any $s_i$ increases, the wall-value sums satisfy
\[
  A(t,R)\le A_{\mathrm{radial}}\le2\pi,\qquad
  B(t,R)\le B_{\mathrm{radial}}\le2\pi.
 \label{eq:wall-angle-necessary}
\]
This is the wall-free angular barrier.

\begin{lemma}[A radius floor for the main core]
\label{lem:main-radius-floor}
If a feasible main-core packing has $t=s_{10}\le1.1265$, then
\[
 R>\frac{15947}{2000}=7.9735.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Non-overlap of circles $9$ and $10$ gives
$s_9+t\ge r_9+r_{10}=3+\sqrt{10}$.  Containment of circle $9$ gives
$R\ge3+s_9$.  Since $\sqrt{10}>3.1$ and $t\le1.1265$,
\[
 R\ge6+\sqrt{10}-t
   \ge6+\sqrt{10}-1.1265
   >6+3.1-1.1265
   =7.9735.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Excluding every radius below the critical value}

Suppose a main-order packing occurs in a tree box.  Its disk-$10$ coordinate
lies in $I$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:main-radius-floor} puts its radius in the
certified derivative range.  If $R<\Rcrit$, then by
\eqref{eq:wall-angle-necessary}, it must satisfy $A(t,R)\le2\pi$ and
$B(t,R)\le2\pi$.

If $t\ge t_{\mathrm{crit}}$, then $A_R<0$ on the interval from $R$ to
$R_{\mathrm{crit}}$, while $A_t>0$ at $R_{\mathrm{crit}}$, so
\[
 A(t,R)>A(t,\Rcrit)
          \ge A(t_{\mathrm{crit}},\Rcrit)=2\pi,
\]
a contradiction.  If $t\le t_{\mathrm{crit}}$, then $B_t<0$ and
$B_R<0$ imply
\[
 B(t,R)>B(t,\Rcrit)
          \ge B(t_{\mathrm{crit}},\Rcrit)=2\pi,
\]
also a contradiction.  These two cases cover every value of $t$.  Thus any
main-order case satisfying the tree's radial hypotheses is impossible below
$\Rcrit$.

\section{The four exceptional cases: a negative-cycle proof}
\label{sec:four-cases}

The four remaining assignments have a different large-circle skeleton and
are not covered by the preceding seven-circle theorem.  Their masks are
$(5,16,40,2)$ (three assignments) and $(4,16,41,2)$ (one assignment).
After projecting away circle $1$, their nine-circle order is
\[
 P=(10,3,7,5,8,4,6,9,2).
\]
The only ambiguity in the relative placement of circles $4$ and $6$ gives
the second possible order
\[
 Q=(10,3,7,5,8,6,4,9,2).
\]
The exact certificate covers both orders; the four assignments in the global
tree project to $P$.

\subsection{From radial boxes to angle-difference constraints}

Fix either order.  For a radial box $s_i\in[\ell_i,u_i]$, containment gives
$0\le s_i\le\Rcap_+-r_i$, where
\[
  \Rcap_+=8.30346812212>\Rcap.
\]
Non-overlap also requires $s_i+s_j\ge r_i+r_j$.  This inequality safely
tightens the lower endpoints of each box.  If any lower endpoint is zero,
the angle lower bound involving that coordinate is set to zero, which is
conservative.

For positive radial endpoints, the cosine forced by a pair of circles is
\[
 c_{ij}(s_i,s_j)=
 \frac{s_i^2+s_j^2-(r_i+r_j)^2}{2s_is_j}.
\]
On each radial rectangle the verifier bounds this expression above by
checking its four corners.  The reduction to corners is valid because, with
one coordinate fixed, this rational function has no interior maximum in the
other coordinate; any stationary point is a minimum.  Square roots of the
integer radii are enclosed by rational lower bounds.

For each pair, a nonnegative rational angle tick $q_{ij}$ is stored.  It is
accepted only after an exact rational Taylor inequality certifies
$\cos(q_{ij})>c_{ij}$ throughout the box.  Floating-point inverse cosine is
used only when searching for candidate ticks; it has no role in replay.
Consequently any feasible angular difference $\Delta_{ij}$ in the fixed
cyclic order must satisfy
\[
  q_{ij}\le \Delta_{ij}\le 2\pi_+-q_{ij},
\]
where $\pi_+$ is a rational upper bound for $\pi$ obtained from Machin's
identity and alternating arctangent series.

Assign the directed edge $j\to i$ weight $-q_{ij}$ and the edge
$i\to j$ weight $2\pi_+-q_{ij}$.  The angle differences are variables
satisfying the corresponding difference constraints.  A directed cycle of
strictly negative total weight is impossible: summing its constraints would
assert that zero is at most a negative number.

\subsection{Finite certificate}

The initial radial domain is covered by rational boxes.  Each unresolved box
is bisected at a rational midpoint; the verifier checks that the children
cover the parent and that no box is duplicated or omitted.  Every leaf has
a recorded negative cycle whose edge weights are recomputed from the radial
box and rational angle ticks.

\section{Composition of the global finite proof}
\label{sec:composition}

The compressed certificate \path{certificates/skeleton-radial-tree-v27.json.gz}
is a complete rational-split tree for the radial domain up to $\Rcap$.  Its
replay checks
\[
  2{,}401\ \text{nodes},\qquad
  1{,}200\ \text{splits},\qquad
  1{,}201\ \text{leaves}.
\]
The associated angular ledgers account for $14{,}757{,}888$ sector
assignments.  Before adding the final geometric theorems, the ledgers had
closed $14{,}757{,}691$ assignments and left $197$ open.  The remaining
assignments divide exactly as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $193$ main-order cases satisfy the wall-free barrier hypotheses;
  \item Four masks project to order $P$; the negative-cycle replay closes
        them.
\end{itemize}
Their common core order is $(10,5,7,9,2,8,6)$, and each disk-$10$ radial
interval is contained in $[0.8588,1.1265]$.
The masks are $(5,16,40,2)$ in three cases and $(4,16,41,2)$ in one.
The composition verifier also reclassifies $145$ core-order terminal models
and $2$ full-order terminal witnesses through the same seven-circle angle
barrier.  This removes their dependence on the previous local terminal rule.
Each of these $147$ cases has the same disk-$10$ interval, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:main-radius-floor} supplies the radius range required by the
barrier.  After these links are replayed, all $14{,}757{,}888$ sector
assignments are closed and no residual case remains.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:global}]
Let $R<\Rcrit$.  Since $\Rcrit<\Rcap$, every putative packing belongs to
the certified radial-tree domain.  Every sector assignment is closed either
by the original exact radial/angular checks, by the main-order barrier, by
the alternative nine-circle certificate, or by the independent
reclassification of the 147 local terminals.  Thus no packing exists below
$\Rcrit$.  Section~\ref{sec:witness} constructs a packing at $\Rcrit$, so
$\Ropt=\Rcrit$.
\end{proof}

\section{A matching ten-circle packing}
\label{sec:witness}

At $(t_{\mathrm{crit}},\Rcrit)$, put circle $10$ at radial distance
$t_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and put each $i\in W$ at its wall radius
$a_i(\Rcrit)=\Rcrit-r_i$.  Use the following polar angles:
\begin{align*}
 \theta_{10}&=0,\\
 \theta_5&=\alpha_5,\\
 \theta_7&=\alpha_5+\beta_{57}=\alpha_7,\\
 \theta_9&=\theta_7+\beta_{79},\\
 \theta_2&=\theta_9+\beta_{92},\\
 \theta_8&=\theta_2+\beta_{28},\\
 \theta_6&=\theta_8+\beta_{86}=2\pi-\alpha_6.
\end{align*}
The identities $\alpha_5+\beta_{57}=\alpha_7$ and
$\alpha_7+C+\alpha_6=2\pi$ are exactly the two root equations.  They make
the eight pairs
\[
 (10,5),(10,7),(10,6),(5,7),(7,9),(9,2),(2,8),(8,6)
\]
tangent.  The remaining 13 pairs within the seven-circle core are
strictly separated throughout the certified root rectangle.

Place the three other centers at the following fixed rational coordinates:
\[
 p_1=(-3.9798,\;6.1148),\qquad
 p_3=(6.4777,\;0.8849),\qquad
 p_4=(5.5222,\;-2.7574).
\]
(The terminating decimals here denote exact rationals.)  The witness replay
checks all 37 non-contact pairs among the ten circles and verifies strict
container inclusion for circles $1,3,4$ and circle $10$, uniformly over the
rational enclosure of $(t_{\mathrm{crit}},\Rcrit)$.  The six circles in $W$
are on the wall, and all remaining inequalities have positive certified
slack.  Hence this is an actual feasible configuration at the exact root,
not merely a rounded numerical picture.

\section{Reproducibility and trust boundary}
\label{sec:verification}

The complete composition is replayed from the repository root with
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
uv run --locked --no-sync python \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}\path{tools/verify_global_optimality_completion.py}
\end{quote}
The first command checks the tree, case linkage, root theorem, witness,
alternative-order certificate, local radius implication, and input hashes,
then writes a JSON report under \path{artifacts/}.

All decisive arithmetic in these replayers is integer or rational arithmetic.
Square roots are enclosed with integer square-root bounds; the trigonometric
inequalities are reduced to rational Taylor bounds and rational enclosures of
$\pi$.  Floating-point routines may help generate candidate split points or
angle ticks, but the verifier rechecks the accepted claims using exact
inequalities.  This separates fast certificate discovery from the smaller
checking computation.

The trust boundary is explicit.  This is a computer-assisted proof whose
soundness depends on the correctness of the Python verifiers, the Python
runtime's integer and \texttt{Fraction} semantics, and the certificate inputs
whose hashes the composition checker records.  The proof is not formalized
end-to-end in Lean.  In particular, references to old Lean developments or
the MPFI generator do not substitute for the exact replay used here.

The repository's global-optimality completion note and the
\path{proof/global_completion/} directory contain the individual theorem
proofs and certificate details.  The composition report records SHA-256
hashes for the tree, certificates, and verifier sources so a replay can be
tied to the checked inputs.

\section{Conclusion and remaining identifications}

The finite tree and its geometric terminal theorems give a global lower bound
at the exact angle-defined value $\Rcrit$.  The root construction supplies a
ten-circle packing at the same value.  Therefore the minimum container radius
is exactly $\Rcrit$, as claimed in Theorem~\ref{thm:global}.

The result does not require an algebraic description of the optimum.  A
separate task would be to prove that $\Rcrit$ equals the previously computed
algebraic root $R_0$; overlapping decimal enclosures alone would not prove
that identity.  The exact coupled-angle definition is already a complete
specification of the optimum.
